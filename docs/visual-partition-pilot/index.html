VTON benchmark

Separating visually distinct garments

Zanone, D&G, and GR10K remain evaluation examples. SAM3 supplies an appearance-based reference: one product may correctly contain two garment-looking regions. These are pseudo-label agreement scores, not human ground-truth accuracy.

Pilot did not pass all acceptance criteria

Measured tradeoff: B recovers 70 of 466 previously missed difficult regions; A recovers 50. B changes small easy-region IoU by -12.6 percentage points and splits per image from 1.41 to 2.38. Neither model clears every pre-set gate; the current model is not replaced.

Two controlled arms · 2000 updates each · 490 validation images · 466 frozen difficult regions.

Backpropagation: A 20.6 minutes; B 17.9 minutes. Teacher generation, feature preparation, both runs, evaluation and speed checks share one fixed two-hour GPU budget.

A uses uniform training images. B emphasizes natural merges/misses plus synthetic layered examples. Both share the corrected objective and starting weights. Baseline is shield epoch 5, originally trained on clothing-only targets; A and B learn the combined clothing/accessory partition. The frozen difficult cohort contains clothing regions, so accessory coverage cannot improve that headline score. Colors align by one-to-one teacher matching; they are visual regions, not fixed clothing classes.

Difficult regions cover less than 2% of the image, touch another foreground region, and had starting IoU below .5; their starting recall is therefore zero by definition. The 29 small easy controls had starting IoU at least .75. Recall counts a region as recovered when its one-to-one matched IoU reaches .5. Only B versus A isolates the effect of the hard-example sampling mixture; both runs also change the loss and increase the starting head from 8 to 24 queries.

A two-hour pilot is a learning signal, not a claim of SAM3 parity. Foreground ownership is exclusive and exhaustive by construction, but foreground accuracy is measured separately. These references combine the fine clothing export with accessory residuals from the four-prompt export. They are not untouched SAM3 masks: this composition can create tiny border fragments, and accessory prompts can also spill onto skin. We retain every reference region in the metrics and show the masks for inspection.

Next candidates to test: associate duplicate clothing/accessory proposals before forming targets, preserve confident easy-region predictions during tuning, and penalize unnecessary splits. The shared loss, target scope and query-count changes are confounded in this pilot, so it does not establish which caused the regressions.
