What the points show

  • Visible collars usually land correctly. All 8 assessable flatlay collar cases and all 9 in each worn view pass. But ordinary neckline bindings are often called collars, and collars are repeatedly called lapels.
  • Sleeves are the strongest category. Of checks where sleeves are visible and assessable, flatlays fail 0/27, real worn photos 1/25, and LeFFA outputs 0/26. This is a small sample, not proof of zero future failures.
  • Shoulders degrade when worn. Straps get replaced by anatomical shoulders, hair or background. Visible-shoulder failures: 1/28 flatlay, 10/28 real worn, 9/27 LeFFA.
  • Other garments contaminate the worn result. The prompt says “the garment” without restricting it to the SKU top, so it also returns buttons and pockets from trousers. That is a failure for this VTON pipeline even when the point is valid for the trousers.

What this means for training

The assumption that Molmo can point reliably on worn images but cannot on flatlays is unsupported here. Flatlay points on existing, visible parts are mostly usable; unrestricted top-10 prompts create many false details in both views.

Do not feed every returned point directly into the DINO loss. First test a product-specific prompt with an explicit “absent” response, keep points within the intended garment, and reject hidden/skin shoulder locations. That is a proposed next experiment; no such filtering was applied to these results.

This does not audit LeFFA’s attention transfer. Molmo was run independently on all three images. The previous two-shoulders-to-one-place transfer failure is a separate mechanism.

Method and limits

Original allenai/Molmo-7B-D-0924, revision cab33fb7f1a40091911f81165f8481920621948f. Exact prompt: Point to all the {plural} on the garment. Ten separate greedy queries per image, 12 maximum crops, 384 maximum new tokens. All raw returned points are audited, before any downstream cap on point count.

The 32 IDs were chosen before inference by a deterministic hash order from 128 complete VITON-HD test triplets. The LeFFA images are existing benchmark outputs. Each view got its own visibility reference: an interior flatlay logo is not assumed visible when worn. Presence and minimum visible instance counts were recorded from unmarked images and hash-locked before the predictions were inspected.

A single assistant then visually judged points. Borderline boundaries, tiny hidden fasteners and uncertain category meanings were excluded from the binary result. Counts are verified visible minima, not exhaustive inventories of tiny parts. There is no independent second rater or human-validated keypoint dataset. The cohort is mostly tops and knits, so it does not establish all-category or all-garment performance.

Definitions are deliberately explicit: a collar is a distinct neck band, a cuff is a distinct sleeve-end band/fold, a lapel is a folded jacket front, and a logo is a brand mark. Plain bindings, ordinary hems, generic prints, other garments and bare skin do not qualify. The reported rate is partly a measure of this semantic/task-scope contract, not only coordinate precision. A point clearly outside a garment boundary fails this strict point audit even if a larger DINO crop around it would still include garment fabric; crop usefulness and downstream loss quality were not measured.
